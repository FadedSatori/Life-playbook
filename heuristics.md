# Heuristics

Rules of thumb that work 80%+ of the time. Faster than full frameworks. Use when you need a quick read.

---

## People

- **First impression rule:** How someone treats service staff is who they are.
- **Belief rule:** When someone shows you who they are the first time, believe them.
- **Words vs. actions:** Watch what people do, not what they say they'll do.
- **Consistency is the tell:** Unreliable people are reliably unreliable. Trust the pattern, not the exception.
- **The third story:** In any conflict, there's your side, their side, and what actually happened. Assume you don't have the full picture.
- **Status signals:** People who need to tell you they're important usually aren't.
- **Energy audit:** After spending time with someone, do you feel charged or drained? That's data.
- **Motive heuristic:** Before accepting advice, ask: what does this person want from this situation?

---

## Decisions

- **The 2-minute rule:** If it takes less than 2 minutes, do it now.
- **The overnight test:** If you're unsure, sleep on it. Almost nothing is as urgent as it feels in the moment.
- **The "tell a friend" test:** If you'd be embarrassed to explain this decision to someone you respect, reconsider.
- **The reversibility test:** Reversible decisions → move fast. Irreversible decisions → slow down, get more data.
- **The subtraction test:** Before adding something, ask if removing something would solve the problem.
- **Default to no:** When unsure, the default answer is no. Let the people and projects worth your yes prove it.
- **The 10x rule:** If you can't find at least one reason the opposite is true, you haven't thought hard enough.

---

## Risk

- **Bet size rule:** Never bet what you can't afford to lose, even on a sure thing.
- **Tail risk:** It's not the average outcome that kills you — it's the worst-case. Model that first.
- **Concentration warning:** When everyone agrees something is safe, ask what they're missing.
- **Skin in the game:** Only take advice from people who bear consequences for being wrong.
- **Variance tolerance:** High variance is only acceptable when the downside is survivable and the upside is life-changing.
- **The ruin condition:** Avoid any strategy that could produce total ruin, no matter how good the expected value.

---

## Time & Energy

- **Meeting filter:** If the meeting has no clear outcome, it's a conversation. Schedule accordingly — or don't.
- **The agenda rule:** Never go to a meeting without knowing why you're there and what a good outcome looks like.
- **The top-3 rule:** Three priorities per day max. More than that means none of them are actually priorities.
- **Energy before time:** Protect your highest-energy hours for your most important work. Clock hours are not equal.
- **The "good enough" threshold:** For low-stakes decisions, good enough is optimal. Spend the extra time on things that matter.
- **Saying no rule:** "No" to one thing is always "yes" to something else. Know what you're trading.
- **Parkinson's Law:** Work expands to fill the time available. Set tighter deadlines.

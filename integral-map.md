# Integral Map

Ken Wilber's Integral Theory is the most comprehensive map of human development currently available. It synthesizes Western psychology, Eastern wisdom traditions, developmental science, systems theory, and philosophy into a single framework — not by averaging them, but by identifying what each captures that the others miss.

This file is a working map for personal use. It is not academic. The goal is application: where are you, where are you going, and what does the full terrain look like.

---

## Why This Map

Every map has blind spots. The problem is not choosing the wrong map — it is using only one.

- Therapy is excellent at the interior individual (trauma, shadow, narrative) and largely ignores the systemic and collective.
- Systems thinking is excellent at exterior collective (organizations, cultures, economies) and largely ignores the interior.
- Buddhism is excellent at states of consciousness and interior development and largely ignores exterior structures.
- Modern neuroscience is excellent at the exterior individual (brain, body) and cannot access the interior directly.

Each approach is partial. Integral Theory provides the meta-framework that holds all of them without flattening any.

The Integral Map's central claim: **any complete account of any human phenomenon requires attending to all four quadrants, all major levels, all lines of development, and the relevant states and types.**

## The Four Quadrants

The foundational structure. Every phenomenon has four irreducible aspects:

|  | **Interior** | **Exterior** |
|---|---|---|
| **Individual** | **Upper Left (I)** — subjective experience, consciousness, feelings, meaning | **Upper Right (It)** — behavior, body, brain, observable actions |
| **Collective** | **Lower Left (We)** — shared culture, mutual understanding, intersubjective meaning | **Lower Right (Its)** — social systems, institutions, economies, built environments |

**The upper left (I):** What is happening in your inner experience — your thoughts, emotions, states, meanings, the quality of awareness itself. This is the domain of psychology, phenomenology, meditation, and shadow work.

**The upper right (It):** What is happening in your body and observable behavior. Brain states, physical training, nutrition, sleep, measurable outputs. This is the domain of neuroscience, medicine, behavioral science, and performance optimization.

**The lower left (We):** What is happening in the shared cultural space between you and others — shared values, language, meaning, norms. This is the domain of culture, relationships, collective identity, and intersubjective understanding.

**The lower right (Its):** What is happening in the systemic and institutional environment — economic systems, social structures, organizations, built environments. This is the domain of sociology, economics, ecology, and systems theory.

**The key insight:** Every significant human problem exists in all four quadrants simultaneously. Treating it in only one quadrant addresses a symptom while leaving the root untouched.

**Example — addiction:**
- Upper Left: the psychological pain, the meaning-making around the substance, the trauma
- Upper Right: the brain chemistry, the physical dependency, the behavioral patterns
- Lower Left: the cultural norms around the substance, the identity of the social group
- Lower Right: the availability of the substance, the economic conditions, the healthcare system

Treating only one quadrant produces partial solutions. AA works primarily in the lower left (community, shared narrative). Medical treatment works in the upper right. Therapy works in the upper left. All are necessary for durable transformation.

**Personal application:**
When working on any aspect of your development — a habit, a relationship, a career move — run it through all four quadrants. What is happening in your inner experience? What is happening in your body and behavior? What is happening in your culture and relationships? What is happening in your systemic environment?

## Levels of Development

Development moves through recognizable stages — both within individuals and across cultures. Different theorists have mapped these differently (Piaget, Kohlberg, Loevinger, Cook-Greuter, Maslow), but they converge on a similar sequence.

Integral Theory uses color coding borrowed from Spiral Dynamics. The colors are labels, not judgments. Every level is appropriate to a specific life condition. None is superior in an absolute sense — though higher levels can solve problems that lower levels cannot.

**The major altitudes:**

**Beige — Archaic/Instinctive:** Pure survival. Food, shelter, safety, reproduction. The base. In crisis states, all humans return here temporarily.

**Purple — Magical/Animistic:** Tribal belonging, ancestor spirits, ritual, kinship bonds. Safety in the group. The individual exists only as member of the tribe.

**Red — Impulsive/Power:** Personal power, dominance, breaking free of tribal constraint. The ego emerges. Might makes right. Short time horizon. Found in: warlords, gangs, young children, conquest empires.

**Blue — Conformist/Order:** Rules, hierarchy, meaning through belonging to the right group, deferred reward. The individual submits to a larger order (nation, religion, organization) in exchange for safety and purpose. Found in: traditional religious institutions, military command structures, early civic societies.

**Orange — Rational/Achievement:** Individual achievement, scientific rationalism, market competition, strategic thinking, empiricism. Success defined by measurable outcomes. Found in: modern capitalism, meritocracy, STEM culture, most Western institutions.

**Green — Pluralistic/Egalitarian:** Sensitivity to all perspectives, rejection of hierarchy, communal values, social justice, ecological awareness. The shadow: relativism that cannot make value judgments; paralysis in the face of necessary hierarchy. Found in: progressive academia, social movements, therapy culture.

**Teal — Integral/Systemic:** Ability to hold multiple levels simultaneously. Systems thinking. Integration of the previous levels rather than rejection of any. The beginning of genuine second-tier thinking. Found in: advanced leadership, complex systems theorists, certain wisdom traditions.

**Turquoise — Holistic:** Collective mind, global systems, integration of spiritual and rational, care for the whole. Rare. Found in: figures like Gandhi, Nelson Mandela (at their best), certain contemplative traditions.

**Critical note:** Most people operate primarily at one or two levels, with the capacity to access adjacent levels in favorable conditions. Development is not linear — stress pulls toward lower levels, growth pulls toward higher ones.

**The key developmental insight:** You cannot skip levels. Blue order must be integrated before Orange rationality is stable. Orange must be integrated before Green pluralism doesn't collapse into relativism. Green must be integrated before Teal integral thinking becomes available. Each level includes and transcends the previous.

## Lines of Development

Development is not uniform across all capacities. A person can be highly developed cognitively (Orange-Teal) while emotionally arrested at Red. A person can be deeply spiritually developed while interpersonally unskilled.

The major developmental lines:
- **Cognitive** — how you think, what you can conceptualize
- **Emotional** — emotional intelligence, self-regulation, empathy
- **Moral** — how you reason about right and wrong
- **Interpersonal** — relational capacity, social intelligence
- **Spiritual** — relationship to transcendent experience and meaning
- **Kinesthetic** — body awareness and physical capability
- **Aesthetic** — developed sense of beauty and form
- **Values** — what you genuinely care about

The playbook addresses multiple lines: cognitive (cross-domain thinking, polymath system), emotional (shadow work, consciousness technologies), moral (warrior codes, stoic practice), physical (health protocols), interpersonal (social tactics), spiritual (hermetic principles, consciousness technologies).

**Application:** Identify your strongest lines and your weakest. Growth usually comes faster from developing the weakest line than from further developing the strongest. A brilliant strategist with poor emotional regulation is limited by the emotional line. Develop it.

## States

States of consciousness are distinct from stages (levels). States are temporary; stages are more permanent structures.

**Natural states:**
- Waking — ordinary consciousness
- Dreaming — REM state
- Deep sleep — delta wave, no ordinary awareness
- Flow — heightened waking performance
- Peak experiences — brief moments of extraordinary clarity or expansion

**Trained states:**
- Meditative states — altered EEG signatures, expanded awareness
- Near-death and psychedelic states — profound structural exposure
- Extreme stress states — narrowed focus, heightened acuity

The relationship between states and stages is important: **a peak state experience gives access to what's possible at higher stages, but does not permanently move you there.** The person who has a transcendent experience in a meditation retreat has not permanently advanced to Turquoise. They have had a preview. Sustained development is what installs the new stage permanently.

The technologies in [consciousness-technologies.md](consciousness-technologies.md) are primarily state-training practices. Used consistently, they gradually reshape the stage structures beneath them.

## Types

Horizontal differences within the same level — personality patterns that persist across development. Myers-Briggs, Enneagram, Big Five, and similar typologies capture types.

Types are not stages. A Red-level personality and a Teal-level personality can both be introverted. A Green-level feminine type and a Green-level masculine type both operate within the same level of development but with different characteristic emphases.

The Integral approach: don't confuse types with stages. The ENFJ who is operating from Orange achieves different (and more limited) outcomes than the ENFJ who is operating from Teal — despite having the same typological profile.

**Practical note:** Type frameworks (especially the Enneagram) are most useful for understanding the characteristic defense mechanisms and growth edges of specific personality structures. Use them as diagnostic tools, not as identities.

## The AQAL Practitioner

AQAL stands for **All Quadrants, All Levels, All Lines, All States, All Types.** It is Wilber's shorthand for the full model.

The AQAL practitioner does not require complete mastery of every line at the highest available level. The goal is:

1. **Cross-quadrant awareness** — being able to see what's happening in all four quadrants, not just one
2. **Developmental literacy** — understanding that people operate from different levels, and meeting them where they are
3. **Multi-line development** — actively developing across cognitive, emotional, interpersonal, spiritual, and physical lines simultaneously
4. **State training** — deliberately developing the capacity to access higher states through regular practice
5. **Type intelligence** — understanding your own type and the types of the people you work with

**The shadow of Integral Theory:** It can become an elaborate intellectual structure used to avoid actual development. Reading this file is not practice. The quadrants must be worked, the lines must be trained, the states must be accessed. The map is not the territory.

Use it as a map. Do the work it points to.

## Where You Are

The honest self-assessment question: **At which level are most of my decisions being made?**

Not which level you understand intellectually. Not which level you aspire to. Where is the center of gravity of your actual choices, your reactive behavior, your automatic responses?

That is your current developmental altitude. Everything above it is territory you can access in favorable conditions and lose in stressful ones. The work is raising the floor, not accessing the peaks.

The quarterly UNIT audit (see [the-unit.md](the-unit.md)) can be run through the Integral lens. For each pillar — physical, mental, social, financial, philosophical — ask: from what level am I currently operating? What would the next level up look like in this domain?

Then do what it takes to get there.

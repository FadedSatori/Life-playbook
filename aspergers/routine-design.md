# Routine Design

A routine for this profile is not about productivity. It is the scaffolding that keeps the energy budget balanced, the sensory load predictable, and the unknowns small — so that what's left of you goes into the things that matter. Design the day around energy, not just time.

> **Evidence key.** `[E: strong]` — consistent RCTs or meta-analyses. `[E: moderate]` — good observational data or small/short trials. `[E: weak]` — mechanism, theory, or qualitative accounts only. `[E: contested]` — serious evidence on both sides. Untagged = practice, framework, or preference, not an empirical claim.

## Principles

1. **Anchors, not a script.** Fix a few points in the day absolutely — wake time, first hour, wind-down — and let the rest flex. Rigid minute-by-minute plans break on the first change and cost more than they save.
2. **Energy before time.** A free hour on the calendar is not free if you'll be at a 1 after the meeting before it. Plan the [energy budget](energy-and-burnout.md#the-energy-model), then the clock.
3. **Recovery goes after the drain, not where there's space.** Schedule it the same way you schedule the demand.
4. **Buffers between everything.** Transitions cost energy (see [Autistic Inertia](energy-and-burnout.md#autistic-inertia)). Fifteen minutes between commitments is part of the plan.
5. **The routine serves you.** When it stops serving you, change it on purpose — in the [Weekly Review](../routines/weekly-review.md), not in the middle of a hard day.

## Adapting the General Daily Routine

Most of [Daily Routine](../routines/daily.md) fits this profile well. A few adjustments:

| Element | Keep / adapt | Why |
|---------|-------------|-----|
| Consistent wake time | **Keep — strongly** | `[E: moderate]` Sleep regularity predicts health independently of duration (see [Daily Routine](../routines/daily.md)), and a fixed start lowers uncertainty for the whole day |
| No phone for the first 30 minutes | **Keep** | Protects you from other people's agenda and from an early sensory and social load |
| Morning light and movement | **Keep** | Choose quiet routes and times if outdoors is crowded |
| Cold exposure | **Optional** | Some find it regulating; for others it is sensory overload before the day starts. Skip it without guilt |
| Written top 3 for the day | **Keep** | Externalizes the plan — see [Executive Function](executive-function.md) |
| 90-minute deep work block | **Keep — protect it** | Deep focus is a strength of this profile; guard the block from interruption |
| Shutdown ritual | **Keep — strongly** | A clear boundary helps transitions; write tomorrow's plan so the day ends closed |
| Social time in the evening | **Adapt** | Only if you have energy left. Recovery first on heavy days |

## A Weekday Template

Adjust the times. Keep the shape.

| Block | What happens | Notes |
|-------|-------------|-------|
| **Wake + anchor (30–60 min)** | Same sequence every day: water, light, movement, breakfast, no phone | The most protected hour of the day |
| **Plan (10 min)** | Top 3, check calendar, body check, energy estimate | Spot the drains and place the recovery |
| **Deep work** | The most important task, uninterrupted | Headphones, notifications off |
| **Buffer (15 min)** | Move, eat, reset | Before every transition into social or meetings |
| **Shallow work / meetings** | Communication, collaboration, admin | Batch them together so the day isn't broken into fragments |
| **Recovery block (20–60 min)** | Quiet, alone, low sensory | After the heaviest part of the day — non-negotiable |
| **Afternoon** | Lighter tasks, interest work, training | |
| **Shutdown (15 min)** | Review, tomorrow's top 3, close everything | |
| **Decompression** | Interest time, solitude, low-demand connection | Before social plans, not after them |
| **Wind-down (60 min)** | Dim, quiet, predictable | See [Sleep](../health/sleep.md) |

## A Weekly Template

| Element | How often | Notes |
|---------|-----------|-------|
| **Weekly review + capacity ledger** | Once | Plan next week's demand vs. recovery — see [The Capacity Ledger](energy-and-burnout.md#the-capacity-ledger) |
| **Admin block** | Once | Every bill, form, and call waits for it — see [Life Admin](executive-function.md#life-admin) |
| **Social quota** | Set your number | Decide in advance how many social commitments a week you can sustain. When the quota is full, the answer is no |
| **Interest blocks** | Several | Protected — see [Special Interests](special-interests.md#protect-the-time) |
| **Low-demand day** | At least half a day | No plans, no obligations, no "should" |
| **Training sessions** | 3–4 | Strength training doubles as proprioceptive regulation — see [Sensory and Health](sensory.md#sensory-and-health) |
| **One connection that doesn't drain you** | At least one | Someone you don't have to mask around |

**The social quota** is the most useful single number on this page. Most overload comes from saying yes one invitation at a time without seeing the total. Pick a number, track it, and protect it.

## Weekends and Days Off

- **Keep the anchors.** Same wake time within an hour. A late weekend start is the easiest way to make Monday hard.
- **Leave real space.** A weekend packed with social plans is not rest for this profile, however enjoyable each plan is.
- **Plan one thing you want to do** — so the day has a shape — and leave the rest open.

---

## When the Routine Breaks

Travel, illness, guests, crises, and holidays will break the routine. That is expected.

- **Keep the smallest version:** wake time, water, one meal you know, one quiet block, bedtime.
- **Rebuild the next day,** not the next week. The routine has no memory of failure.
- **Expect the cost.** A broken routine drains more than it seems to. Plan a recovery day after.
- See [Uncertainty and Change](uncertainty-and-change.md) for handling the disruption itself.

---

## Seasonal and Life Changes

Routines that worked last year may not work this year. New job, new home, new relationship, a child, a season with less light — each changes the budget.

- **Review the routine quarterly,** not only when it fails.
- **Change one element at a time** and give it two weeks before judging it.
- **Watch the energy log** — it will tell you a routine has stopped working before you consciously notice.

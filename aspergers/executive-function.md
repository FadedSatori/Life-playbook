# Executive Function

Planning, starting, switching, remembering, and estimating time are the brain's management layer — and for many people with the Asperger's profile, it runs on manual. Don't rely on the manager. Build the system outside your head.

> **Evidence key.** `[E: strong]` — consistent RCTs or meta-analyses. `[E: moderate]` — good observational data or small/short trials. `[E: weak]` — mechanism, theory, or qualitative accounts only. `[E: contested]` — serious evidence on both sides. Untagged = practice, framework, or preference, not an empirical claim.

## What It Is and Why It's Hard

Executive function is the set of skills that manage the rest: planning, starting tasks, switching between them, holding things in working memory, inhibiting impulses, and tracking time.

`[E: moderate]` A meta-analysis found autistic people show executive-function differences across all of these areas, at a moderate average size (Demetriou et al., 2018). The differences are real and vary widely between people. If ADHD is also present — about a quarter or more of autistic people (see [What Comes With It](overview.md#what-comes-with-it)) — executive function is usually the hardest-hit area.

None of this says anything about intelligence. Many people with this profile can design a complex system and still miss the dentist appointment. Both are true at once, and that gap is what this file is for.

## The Core Rule: Externalize Everything

Working memory is small and leaks. Anything that lives only in your head will be dropped eventually — usually at the worst time.

- **One capture point.** A single inbox — notebook, app, or text file — where every task, idea, and commitment goes the moment it appears. One, not five.
- **One calendar.** Every time-bound thing, with an alarm.
- **One list per context** — errands, calls, computer, home — so you can act without re-sorting.
- **Review weekly.** Empty the inbox into lists and the calendar during the [Weekly Review](../routines/weekly-review.md).

If it's not written down, it doesn't exist.

## Starting: Make the First Step Physical

Vague tasks don't start. "Do taxes" has no first move. "Open the folder labeled Taxes and find last year's return" does.

**For any stuck task:**
1. Write the very first *physical* action — what your hands do in the first 60 seconds.
2. Make it small enough that it feels slightly silly.
3. Do only that. Momentum usually follows; if it doesn't, the first step was still too big.

See [Autistic Inertia](energy-and-burnout.md#autistic-inertia) for transition warnings, starting rituals, and body doubling.

## Checklists for Anything Recurring

If you do it more than twice, write the checklist once and stop re-thinking it every time.

**Good candidates:** leaving the house, packing for travel, the morning routine, end of the workday, weekly cleaning, monthly bills, preparing for a meeting.

**Example — leaving the house:**
- [ ] Keys, wallet, phone
- [ ] Headphones / sensory kit
- [ ] Water
- [ ] Anything on the launch pad
- [ ] Stove off, door locked

**The launch pad:** one fixed spot by the door. Anything that needs to leave the house tomorrow goes there tonight.

## Time

Many people with this profile underestimate how long things take, and lose track of time inside deep focus.

- **Estimate, then multiply by 1.5.** Track a few actual times to calibrate your own multiplier.
- **Visible time:** an analog clock or visual timer where you work. Time you can see is easier to feel.
- **Alarms for leaving, not arriving.** Set the alarm for when you need to stop and get ready, not for the appointment itself.
- **Buffers between commitments.** Back-to-back is a plan that only works if nothing runs over.
- **Tunnel protection:** when you go deep, set an alarm for meals and the next commitment before you start.

## Fewer Decisions

Every decision draws on the same [energy budget](energy-and-burnout.md#the-energy-model). Cut the number you make.

- **Defaults:** a short rotation of meals, a standard grocery list, the same breakfast.
- **A uniform:** a few outfits you like and that feel right on your skin (see [Sensory](sensory.md)).
- **Rules instead of choices:** "I reply to email at 11 and 4." "I say no to events on weeknights."
- **Pre-decide the hard ones** in your calm hours, using the decision filter in [Core Principles](../principles.md).

## Life Admin

Bills, forms, phone calls, appointments, and paperwork pile up because each one is small, boring, and ambiguous — the exact combination that is hardest to start.

- **One admin block a week,** same day and time. Everything admin waits for it.
- **Automate money:** autopay bills, automatic savings and investing (see [Finances](../career/finances.md)).
- **Templates** for emails you send often — requests, follow-ups, declines.
- **Phone calls:** write a script first — who you are, what you need, the one question you must get answered. Ask for an email follow-up. Use email or online forms instead whenever possible.
- **Done is better than perfect.** Most forms don't need your best work. They need to be submitted.

## Tools

Use whatever reduces load. Common ones:
- A calendar with alarms, and a task app with recurring tasks
- Visual timers
- Notes on your phone for scripts and checklists
- Writing assistants for drafting difficult or tedious messages — then edit in your own voice
- Body doubling — work sessions alongside someone else, in person or on a video call

The system matters more than the tool. Pick one of each and stay with it for three months before switching.

## When to Get Assessed for ADHD

If executive function is the biggest obstacle in your life — despite good systems — consider an ADHD assessment. ADHD is common alongside this profile, and its treatments, including medication, have strong evidence `[E: strong]` for adults. See [Health and Care](health-and-care.md).

# Energy and Burnout

The Asperger's profile runs on a tighter energy budget than the world assumes, and pays a hidden surcharge on ordinary days. Manage the budget deliberately — or burnout will manage it for you.

> **Evidence key.** `[E: strong]` — consistent RCTs or meta-analyses. `[E: moderate]` — good observational data or small/short trials. `[E: weak]` — mechanism, theory, or qualitative accounts only. `[E: contested]` — serious evidence on both sides. Untagged = practice, framework, or preference, not an empirical claim. Burnout, meltdown, and inertia are defined mostly by autistic adults' own accounts; `weak` here means "qualitative," not "doubtful."

## The Energy Model

Every day starts with a finite budget. Social interaction, sensory load, masking, unexpected change, task switching, and decisions all draw on it. Sleep, solitude, deep interests, and predictable routine refill it.

The "spoon" model (Christine Miserandino, 2003 — originally written about chronic illness) is widely used in autistic communities for the same idea. It is a working model, not a measured quantity. But it predicts real behavior: the day that looked easy on the calendar and left you wrecked usually had costs the calendar didn't show.

**Common drains:**

| Drain | Why it costs | Example |
|-------|-------------|---------|
| Masking | Running a second process on top of the first | The networking event, the job interview |
| Sensory load | Constant input that doesn't get filtered out | Open office, supermarket, fluorescent light |
| Unexpected change | The plan in your head has to be torn down and rebuilt ([more](uncertainty-and-change.md)) | Meeting moved, plans canceled last minute |
| Task switching | Deep focus is expensive to redirect | Constant chat interruptions |
| Ambiguity | Unspoken expectations have to be guessed, then second-guessed | Vague instructions, "let's play it by ear" |
| Real-time social processing | Reading faces, tone, and subtext while also talking | Group conversation |
| Decisions | Each one draws on the same pool | The restaurant menu at the end of a long day |

**Common restorers:** solitude, time in a deep interest, predictable routine, movement, sleep, stimming, deep pressure, low-sensory spaces, and time with people you don't have to mask around.

## Masking Has a Price

Masking — also called camouflaging — is suppressing autistic traits and performing non-autistic ones: forcing eye contact, scripting conversation, copying other people's expressions, hiding stims, and not reacting to sounds or lights that hurt. It works. It also costs energy you don't get back until you're alone.

`[E: moderate]` Autistic adults who camouflage more report more anxiety and depression (Hull et al., 2021), and camouflaging has been linked to suicidal thoughts (Cassidy et al., 2018). Most of this research is cross-sectional, so cause and effect likely run in both directions — but long-term heavy masking is a recognized risk, not a neutral social skill.

**The rule:** masking is a tool, not a default.
- **Spend it where the return is real** — the interview, the negotiation, the first meeting that matters.
- **Budget recovery after it,** the way you budget recovery after a hard training session.
- **Shrink the number of places you have to mask at all.** Where it is safe, unmask in increments: let yourself stim, drop the forced eye contact, say "I need a minute."
- **Watch for losing yourself under it.** After years of heavy masking, many people lose track of what they actually like, want, and feel. [Solitude](../solitude.md) and honest journaling help you find the line again.

## Overload, Meltdown, Shutdown

Overload is the cause. Meltdown and shutdown are two ways the system responds when input exceeds capacity. `[E: weak]` (described consistently by autistic adults and clinicians; little controlled research)

- **Meltdown** — outward. An involuntary loss of control: crying, shouting, pacing, an overwhelming need to escape. It is not a tantrum. There is no goal, and it cannot be reasoned with while it is happening.
- **Shutdown** — inward. Going quiet, losing speech, freezing, withdrawing, unable to process. Easy for others to miss, or to misread as sulking or indifference.

Neither is a character failure. Both are a fuse blowing.

**Early warning signs** (common ones — write your own in [Your Profile](overview.md#your-profile)):
- Irritability out of proportion to the moment
- Sounds or light suddenly painful
- Words getting harder to find
- Clumsiness
- A strong urge to leave
- Replaying conversations on a loop
- Forgetting to eat or drink

**During:**
1. **Reduce input.** Leave, darken, quiet. Headphones on.
2. **Stop talking and stop deciding.** Don't try to reason your way through it.
3. **Use your pre-written exit line:** "I need to step out for a bit. I'm okay." Write it in advance — you may not have words in the moment.
4. **Regulate the body:** deep pressure, rocking, walking, a familiar stim or object.
5. **Wait.** It passes.

**After:** expect a hangover — tiredness, low mood, sometimes embarrassment. Sleep and low demand first. No analysis until you've recovered. Then a short, blame-free debrief: What was the load stack? What was the first warning sign? What could have been cut earlier? (Same spirit as the debrief in [Failure](../failure.md) — information, not judgment.)

**What helps from other people** (put this in your [User Manual](user-manual-template.md)): fewer words, space, no touch unless you ask, no demand for an explanation right now, and a quiet "I'm here when you're ready."

## Autistic Burnout

`[E: weak]` Autistic burnout is not in the DSM. It was defined from autistic adults' own accounts (Raymaker et al., 2020; Higgins et al., 2021): pervasive, long-term exhaustion, loss of function, and reduced tolerance to stimulus — typically lasting three months or more — caused by chronic life stress and a mismatch between what is expected of you and what you can sustain, without adequate support.

**Signs:**
- Exhaustion that sleep doesn't fix
- Skills fading — speaking, planning, self-care, tolerating noise
- Sensory tolerance dropping sharply
- More frequent meltdowns or shutdowns
- Masking becoming impossible
- Withdrawing from almost everything

**Burnout or depression?** They overlap and can happen together. In autistic adults' accounts, burnout centers on exhaustion and lost capacity, and it eases with withdrawal and reduced demands. Depression centers on low mood, loss of pleasure, and hopelessness, and withdrawal alone does not fix it. If loss of pleasure or hopelessness is prominent, see a clinician — ideally one who understands autism (see [Health and Care](health-and-care.md#mental-health-support)).

**Recovery protocol** (drawn from what autistic adults report helps):
1. **Cut demands hard** — work, social, obligations — to the minimum. Use leave or accommodations if you have them.
2. **Unmask fully** somewhere safe.
3. **Sensory retreat:** quiet, dim, comfortable, predictable.
4. **Deep interests with no productivity goal.**
5. **Sleep and regular food on reminders** — internal signals may be offline. See [Sleep](../health/sleep.md).
6. **Return slowly.** Reintroduce one demand at a time and watch for the warning signs coming back.
7. **Change the conditions that caused it.** Burnout repeats if the mismatch stays.

Recovery takes weeks to months. Treat it like an injury, not a bad week.

**When it gets dark.** `[E: moderate]` (large national register study) Autistic adults — especially those without intellectual disability — die by suicide at several times the rate of the general population (Hirvikoski et al., 2016). If thoughts of not wanting to be here show up, treat them as an emergency signal, not a verdict on you: tell someone you trust today. In the US, call or text 988; elsewhere, your local crisis line. Write a [crisis plan](health-and-care.md#write-a-crisis-plan-while-youre-well) while you're well.

## Autistic Inertia

`[E: weak]` Difficulty starting, stopping, or switching activities — not laziness and not a lack of motivation (Buckle et al., 2021, from autistic adults' accounts). It is the cost of deep focus showing up at the edges.

**Tools:**
- **Transition warnings:** alarms at 10 minutes and 2 minutes before a switch, not just at the switch
- **Starting rituals:** the same small sequence every time (same drink, same playlist, same first action)
- **Body doubling:** working alongside someone, in person or on a call — presence alone helps many people start
- **Buffer time** between scheduled things; back-to-back is expensive
- **Stop at a clean point** — leave a note for where you'll restart, so stopping costs less

## Building It Into the Routine

**Daily:** plan with an energy budget, not only a time budget. Put recovery blocks after known drains, not wherever there's a gap. See [Daily Routine](../routines/daily.md).

**Body checks on a schedule** — three times a day, since the signals may not arrive on their own:
- Hungry? Thirsty? Need the bathroom?
- Too hot or cold? Tense anywhere?
- Tired? Overloaded? What am I feeling — and if I can't name it, what is my body doing?

**Weekly energy log** — add to the [Weekly Review](../routines/weekly-review.md):

| Day | Biggest drain | Biggest restorer | Energy at day's end (1–5) | Warning signs? |

**Watch the trend, not the day.** Three weeks of sliding scores is a burnout warning. Cut load then — not when you can no longer function.

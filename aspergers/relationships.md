# Relationships

Close relationships ask for the hardest things this profile does — reading unspoken needs, sharing space, tolerating unpredictability — and give back some of the most important things in a life. Make the implicit explicit, protect your recovery, and build relationships that fit both people's wiring.

## The Core Principle: Make the Implicit Explicit

Most relationship friction for this profile comes from expectations nobody said out loud — how often to text, what "I'm fine" means, whether a bad mood is about you. Each one is small. Together they are exhausting for both people.

**Write the operating agreement together.** Not a contract — a shared understanding, revisited every few months:
- How often do we want to be in touch when apart, and how?
- How does each of us signal "I need alone time," and what does it mean? (Not rejection.)
- How do we raise a problem? Right away, or at a set time?
- What does each of us need after a hard day?
- What does each of us count as care?

Share your [User Manual](user-manual-template.md). Ask your partner or friend to write one too.

## Mixed-Neurotype Relationships

When one person is autistic and the other isn't, the [double empathy](social-decoding.md#double-empathy-the-problem-runs-both-ways) gap runs through the middle of the relationship. Neither person is the broken one. Both are translating.

**What tends to help:**
- **Name the difference without blame.** "I'm not ignoring your hint — I genuinely didn't catch it. Can you say it straight?"
- **Agree on direct words for important things.** The partner who communicates in hints learns to say it plainly when it matters. You learn to check in when the signals are unclear.
- **Ask, don't guess, about tone.** "Are you upset with me, or is it something else?" beats a day of silent worry.
- **Recognize each other's effort.** Your partner explaining things directly is effort. You reading subtext is effort. Say thank you for it.

## Love in Different Languages

`[E: weak]` Many autistic adults describe showing care through practical help, problem-solving, sharing interests and information, and reliable presence — sometimes more than through words or physical affection.

- **Translate your care so it lands.** "I fixed the shelf because I know it's been bugging you." "I sent you that article because it made me think of what you said."
- **Learn their language too.** If they need words, say them — even when it feels redundant. If they need touch, find the forms of touch that work for you.
- **Make lists.** "Things that make my partner feel loved" — keep it on your phone. Remembering is not the same as caring less; it is a system serving the care.

See [Intimate Partnership](../relationships/intimate-partnership.md) for the broader framework.

## Alone Time Is Maintenance, Not Rejection

Solitude restores your [energy budget](energy-and-burnout.md). Without it, you have less to bring to the relationship.

- **Make it a standing agreement,** not a request you have to justify every time.
- **Name it clearly:** "I'm going to recharge for an hour. It's not about you — I'll be better company after."
- **Parallel time counts.** Being in the same room, each doing your own thing, is connection. Tell your partner you experience it that way.
- **Protect it in shared living:** a room or corner that is yours, headphones that mean "not now," a quiet hour after work.

See [Solitude](../solitude.md).

## Sharing a Home

- **Sensory needs are household design questions,** not personal failings: lighting, noise, smells, clutter, temperature. Negotiate them like any other shared decision. See [Sensory](sensory.md).
- **Separate sleep spaces are legitimate** if sound, light, temperature, or touch make shared sleep hard. Good sleep makes for a better relationship, not a weaker one.
- **Routines and chores in writing** — who does what, when. Ambiguity about chores causes more conflict than the chores themselves.
- **Advance notice for visitors** and changes to household plans.

## Physical Intimacy and Sex

- **Touch preferences are sensory preferences.** Light touch may be unpleasant; firm pressure may be good. Some fabrics, sounds, and smells may be distracting. Tell your partner specifically.
- **Explicit communication is an asset here.** Asking "Do you like this?" and saying "Firmer, please" or "Not that" is good practice for everyone, and a strength of this profile.
- **Predictability can help.** Some people find it easier when intimacy isn't a surprise.
- **Overload ends desire.** If you're depleted, sensory-overloaded, or anxious, arousal is unlikely. That is physiology, not a verdict on the relationship.

See [Sexuality](../sexuality.md).

## Conflict

- **Pause when flooded.** You can't repair while overloaded. "I want to sort this out. I need an hour to calm down first." See [Conflict and Repair](social-decoding.md#conflict-and-repair).
- **Ask what they heard** before defending what you meant.
- **Writing helps many people.** A letter or long message lets you process at your own pace. Say the warm part explicitly — tone doesn't travel in text.
- **The delayed wave:** you may only realize hours later how you felt about a conflict. It is fine to come back to it. See [Emotions](emotions.md#the-delayed-signal).
- **Watch for literal-meaning traps.** "You never help" is usually not a claim about every instance. Respond to the feeling, not the statistic.

## Dating

- **Text first can be an advantage.** Apps and messaging let you communicate in writing before meeting.
- **Be clear about what you want.** Direct statements about intentions save both people time and are often appreciated.
- **Choose the setting:** quiet, familiar, time-limited, with an activity to share — a walk, a museum, a game.
- **Disclosure timing is yours.** Some people disclose early to filter for acceptance; others wait until trust is built. Either is legitimate.
- **Watch for exploitation.** `[E: moderate]` Autistic adults report more experiences of manipulation and abuse (see [Protecting Yourself](social-decoding.md#protecting-yourself)). Judge by actions over words, especially early. Be wary of anyone who rushes intimacy, isolates you, or asks for money.

## Friendship

- **Fewer, deeper friendships are a valid shape.** You don't need a large circle.
- **Interest-based friendships** carry themselves: the shared activity does the work. See [Finding Your People](social-decoding.md#finding-your-people).
- **Schedule contact.** A recurring reminder to message someone is not fake. It is how the friendship gets maintained. See [Dunbar's Layers](../relationships/principles.md#network-architecture-dunbars-layers).
- **Say what you value about them.** People with this profile often feel more than they express. Say it.

---

## Family

- **Boundaries with family are allowed,** including around holidays, visits, and how long you stay.
- **Plan family events like any high-load event:** know the schedule, have a quiet room, set a leave time, bring your [sensory kit](sensory.md#the-sensory-kit).
- **Old roles return fast.** Family may still treat you as the person you were before you understood yourself. You don't have to re-explain everything. You can just act on what you know now.
- **Late recognition can reframe family history.** See [Identity and Unmasking](identity-and-unmasking.md).

# Reading List

The best way into this profile is through the people who live it, and the clinicians and historians who took it seriously. A short, curated list — memoir, practical guides, and history — with one line on why each earns its place.

Part of the [Asperger's](overview.md) section. For how to read for retention and change, see [Reading System](../learning/reading-system.md).

---

## Before You Start

- **A memoir is one life, not a manual.** Read for recognition — the lines that make you think "that's me" — and leave the rest.
- **Older books use older language** ("Asperger's syndrome," "disorder," functioning labels). That's history, not a reason to skip them.
- **Keep a recognition list.** Write down every passage that describes you precisely. It becomes material for [Your Profile](overview.md#your-profile) and your [User Manual](user-manual-template.md).

## Start Here

| Book | Why read it |
|------|-------------|
| Tony Attwood, *The Complete Guide to Asperger's Syndrome* (2007) | The standard clinical reference, written with real respect for the people it describes. Read the chapters that match your questions, not cover to cover. |
| Cynthia Kim, *Nerdy, Shy, and Socially Inappropriate: A User Guide to an Asperger Life* (2014) | A practical, funny guide from an adult diagnosed in her forties. Closest in spirit to this section. |
| Devon Price, *Unmasking Autism* (2022) | The clearest account of masking, its cost, and how to take it off — with exercises. |

## Memoir: The View From Inside

| Book | Why read it |
|------|-------------|
| Liane Holliday Willey, *Pretending to Be Normal: Living with Asperger's Syndrome* (1999) | An early account of masking, written before the word was in common use. |
| John Elder Robison, *Look Me in the Eye: My Life with Asperger's* (2007) | Diagnosed at 40; a life of building things, misreading people, and finally understanding why. |
| Daniel Tammet, *Born on a Blue Day* (2006) | Asperger's alongside synesthesia and exceptional memory, told with unusual clarity. |
| Temple Grandin, *Thinking in Pictures* (1995) | A visual thinker's account of how a different mind works — and how she built a career around it. |
| Rudy Simone, *Aspergirls* (2010) | Written for and about women and girls with Asperger's, a group long missed by diagnosis. |

## Practical Guides

| Book | Why read it |
|------|-------------|
| Michael John Carley, *Asperger's from the Inside Out* (2008) | A guide for adults newly diagnosed, written by one: what to do in the first year after recognition. |
| Barry Prizant, *Uniquely Human* (2015) | Written mostly about children, but its core idea — autistic behavior as meaningful, not as symptoms to extinguish — reframes everything. Good to give to family. |

## History and Context

| Book | Why read it |
|------|-------------|
| Steve Silberman, *NeuroTribes* (2015) | The history of how autism and Asperger's came to be understood, and misunderstood. The best single book on why the categories look the way they do. |
| Edith Sheffer, *Asperger's Children: The Origins of Autism in Nazi Vienna* (2018) | The documented history behind the name. Hard reading, and necessary context for [The Name's History](overview.md#aspergers-and-autism-the-distinction). |

## Research to Follow

- ***Autism in Adulthood*** — a peer-reviewed journal focused on autistic adults, including many of the studies cited in this section.
- **AASPIRE** (Academic Autism Spectrum Partnership in Research and Education) — research done in partnership with autistic adults, including the work defining autistic burnout and studies of healthcare access.

When you read a new study, run it through the [evidence key](overview.md): is it a trial, a survey, or interviews? How many people? Who was studied? See [Epistemic Self-Defense](../epistemic-self-defense.md).

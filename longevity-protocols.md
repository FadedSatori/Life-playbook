# Longevity Protocols

The goal: not just to live long, but to be physically and cognitively exceptional at 70, 80, 90. Healthspan over lifespan. The interventions with the most evidence, ranked by leverage.

*Built on work from Peter Attia (Outlive), Andrew Huberman, Rhonda Patrick, David Sinclair, and Morgan Levine.* Sinclair's specific claims (resveratrol, NMN) are among the most disputed in the field — treat them accordingly.

> **Evidence key.** `[E: strong]` — consistent RCTs or meta-analyses. `[E: moderate]` — good observational data or small/short trials. `[E: weak]` — mechanism, animal data, or expert opinion only. `[E: contested]` — serious evidence on both sides. Untagged = practice or preference, not an empirical claim. Most longevity evidence is observational by necessity — nobody runs a 50-year RCT — so `moderate` is the ceiling for many claims here.

## The Four Horsemen — What Actually Kills You

Among nonsmokers over 50, roughly 80% of deaths come from four disease categories (Attia's framing, *Outlive*):
1. Cardiovascular disease
2. Cancer
3. Neurodegenerative disease (Alzheimer's, dementia)
4. Metabolic dysfunction (type 2 diabetes and its cascade)

Everything in this file targets the *upstream causes* of these four — years and decades before they manifest. The key insight: these diseases are heavily lifestyle-influenced `[E: strong]` and develop over decades. The interventions that work must be installed decades before the symptoms appear.

**The timing problem:** Most people don't think seriously about longevity until they're sick or watching someone else get sick. By then, 20-30 years of compounding damage has been done. The person who started exercising at 25 will be in a categorically different position at 65 than the person who started at 45, even if they do identical things from 45 onward. Start now, regardless of current age.

## The #1 Longevity Lever: Cardiorespiratory Fitness

`[E: moderate]` **VO2 max** — the maximum rate at which your body can use oxygen during exercise — is one of the strongest predictors of all-cause mortality. In 122,000 patients who did treadmill testing (Mandsager et al., 2018), the lowest-fitness group had roughly 5x the mortality risk of the elite group, a gap larger than smoking or diabetes. This is observational — fitness partly reflects underlying health — but the trial evidence that training raises VO2 max is strong.

**How to build it:**
- **Zone 2 cardio:** 150–180 min/week of low-intensity steady-state (conversational pace, nose breathing possible). Builds mitochondrial density — the engine of every cell. Substrate: fat oxidation. Marker: you can hold a full conversation. Zone 2 is the foundation.
- **VO2 max intervals:** 1–2x/week. 4 min at maximum sustainable effort (approximately 8–9 on perceived exertion), 3 min active recovery, 4–6 rounds (the Norwegian 4x4; see [fitness.md](health/fitness.md)). This raises the ceiling. Must be uncomfortable.

**Tracking VO2 max:** Wearables (Garmin, Polar, Apple Watch) provide estimates. Lab testing (metabolic cart) is more accurate. For practical purposes, track approximate fitness level against age-adjusted tables. Target: at minimum "above average" for your age. Target: "elite" for your age cohort, which would put you in the top 2.5%.

**The 40-year projection:** `[E: moderate]` Your VO2 max declines approximately 10% per decade after 30, even without intervention. With consistent Zone 2 training, decline can be slowed to 5% per decade. The person who starts with a high VO2 max and maintains the training has cardiovascular capacity at 70 that looks like an untrained 50-year-old.

## Strength Training as Longevity Protocol

`[E: moderate]` Muscle mass and strength are strong predictors of longevity after 50. Sarcopenia (muscle loss with age) accelerates every disease process. The people who maintain muscle into old age live longer and live better — with dramatically lower rates of metabolic disease, falls, and cognitive decline.

**Targets for longevity (not aesthetics):**
- **Grip strength:** `[E: moderate]` The single best simple proxy for overall muscle health and cardiovascular mortality. A sustained grip below gender/age norms is a strong independent predictor of early death.
- **Hip hinge strength (deadlift pattern):** Protects the posterior chain, prevents falls, maintains power generation from the hips
- **Carry capacity (farmer's carry, loaded walks):** Functional strength that directly translates to real-world capability
- **Overhead pressing:** Shoulder health and functional overhead reach — critical for avoiding the stooped posture of aging
- **Single-leg stability:** Balance, fall prevention, knee and hip health. Falls are one of the primary causes of mortality-accelerating injury in older adults.

**The reverse deceleration:** `[E: moderate]` Muscle is lost at approximately 3–8% per decade after 30 without resistance training, accelerating after 60. Consistent resistance training slows this substantially and builds muscle at any age, including in people in their 80s and 90s `[E: strong]`.

**Minimum effective dose:** 3 sessions/week, compound movements, progressive overload. The key word is progressive — the load must increase over time for adaptation to continue. See [fitness.md](health/fitness.md) for the full periodization protocol.

## Sleep as the Primary Recovery Protocol

Every longevity protocol fails without sleep. See [sleep.md](health/sleep.md) for the full protocol.

**Longevity-specific mechanisms:**
- **Amyloid clearance:** `[E: contested]` The glymphatic model (see [sleep.md](health/sleep.md)) proposes deep sleep flushes amyloid beta and tau. One night of total sleep deprivation produces a small (~5%) measurable increase in brain amyloid (Shokri-Kojori et al., 2018). Short sleep is associated with higher dementia risk `[E: moderate]`, though the direction of cause is not settled.
- **Growth hormone release:** `[E: strong]` The majority of GH secretion occurs during the first slow-wave sleep cycles. Critical for cellular repair, muscle recovery, and metabolic health. Disrupting sleep structure (alcohol is the most common disruptor of slow-wave sleep) suppresses GH.
- **Immune function:** `[E: moderate]` One night of partial sleep deprivation (awake 3–7am) reduced natural killer cell activity by about 28%, returning to normal after recovery sleep (Irwin et al., 1994). Links between short sleep and cancer risk are `[E: weak]` — inconsistent across studies.
- **Telomere maintenance:** `[E: weak]` Sleep deprivation is associated with faster telomere shortening — one of the molecular hallmarks of aging.

7–9 hours is not optional. It is medicine.

## Metabolic Health — The Foundation

**Insulin sensitivity** `[E: moderate]` is the metabolic master variable. Poor insulin sensitivity is upstream of cardiovascular disease, cancer, neurodegeneration, and obesity. The alarming statistic: only about 7% of American adults have optimal cardiometabolic health across adiposity, blood pressure, glucose, lipids, and cardiovascular disease (O'Hearn et al., 2022).

**What controls it:**
- Exercise `[E: strong]` (the most powerful lever — muscle is the primary glucose sink; resistance training is particularly powerful)
- Sleep `[E: moderate]` (short-term sleep restriction reduces insulin sensitivity by roughly 15–25% in healthy adults)
- Diet composition (minimize ultra-processed food, refined carbohydrates, vegetable oils; see [nutrition.md](health/nutrition.md))
- Time-restricted eating (8–10 hour eating window — the evidence for longer fasting is thinner)

**Biomarkers to track (annually minimum):** Many targets below are stricter than standard clinical cutoffs; they reflect longevity-medicine opinion `[E: weak]` unless tagged. ApoB as the best lipid risk marker is `[E: strong]`.
- Fasting glucose (target: 70–90 mg/dL; risk begins above 100)
- HbA1c (target: below 5.4%; pre-diabetes begins at 5.7%)
- Triglycerides (target: below 100 mg/dL; under 70 is better)
- HDL cholesterol (target: above 40–60 mg/dL; very high HDL is *not* extra protective, and raising HDL with drugs failed in trials)
- Triglycerides/HDL ratio (target: below 1.0 — the best simple insulin resistance proxy)
- Waist circumference (best non-blood metabolic proxy; target: below 37" men, 31.5" women)
- ApoB (the lipoprotein marker that most accurately predicts cardiovascular risk; better than LDL-C; target: below 80 mg/dL for high longevity, below 60 if history of cardiovascular disease)
- hs-CRP (high-sensitivity C-reactive protein; inflammation marker; target: below 1.0 mg/L)

## Hormesis — Stress That Strengthens

Brief, controlled exposure to stressors that activate the body's adaptive response. The biology of antifragility.

**Cold exposure:**
- `[E: weak]` Cold shower: 2–3 min, 3–4x/week. Activates brown adipose tissue (BAT), raises norepinephrine; mood and metabolic benefits are small and mostly from short studies.
- `[E: weak]` Cold plunge: the "11 min/week" figure comes from one observational study of Scandinavian winter swimmers (Søberg et al., 2021), not a dose-finding trial. A 1-hour immersion at 14°C raised norepinephrine ~5x and dopamine ~2.5x (Šrámek et al., 2000); shorter plunges produce smaller rises.
- `[E: moderate]` Avoid cold immersion immediately after strength training — it blunts muscle growth (Roberts et al., 2015). After endurance work it is less of a problem.

**Heat exposure (sauna):**
- `[E: moderate]` 4–7x/week vs. once a week → ~50% lower fatal cardiovascular disease and ~40% lower all-cause mortality (Laukkanen et al., 2015; ~2,300 Finnish men, ~20-year follow-up). Observational — sauna users may be healthier to begin with.
- Mechanism `[E: weak]`: cardiovascular conditioning (heart rate reaches 120–150 bpm), heat shock protein activation, blood pressure reduction. The often-quoted 16x growth hormone spike came from an extreme protocol (two 1-hour sessions at 80°C in one day) and has no shown effect on muscle or longevity.
- `[E: moderate]` Cognitive: 4–7x/week was associated with ~65% lower dementia risk (same Finnish cohort, same observational caveat)

**Fasting:**
- Time-restricted eating (16:8) — `[E: moderate]` safe for most healthy adults; benefits beyond simply eating less are small (see [nutrition.md](health/nutrition.md))
- `[E: weak]` Extended fasting (24–72 hours, 2–4x/year) — activates autophagy in animal models; human autophagy and longevity benefits are unproven (cellular self-cleaning), mTOR suppression, metabolic reset; requires medical consideration if taking medications or with metabolic disease

## The Hallmarks of Aging

Modern longevity science (Lopez-Otin et al.) identifies 12 hallmarks of aging — the cellular and molecular processes that produce biological deterioration. The lifestyle interventions in this file target multiple hallmarks simultaneously:

- **Genomic instability** — addressed by sleep (DNA repair during sleep), antioxidants, avoiding environmental carcinogens
- **Telomere attrition** — slowed by exercise, sleep, stress management; accelerated by chronic psychological stress and sleep deprivation
- **Epigenetic alterations** — exercise, sleep, and diet quality directly influence epigenetic age (biological age vs. chronological age)
- **Mitochondrial dysfunction** — Zone 2 cardio is the primary intervention; directly builds new mitochondria
- **Cellular senescence** — `[E: weak]` exercise and fasting may both stimulate clearance of senescent cells (zombie cells that drive inflammation)
- **Chronic inflammation** — addressed by diet quality, sleep, exercise, and stress management; the common pathway through which most longevity-shortening behaviors cause damage

The Morgan Levine biological age clock ("Phenotypic Age"): a blood test (phenoAge) estimates biological age from inflammatory and metabolic markers. `[E: weak]` Useful for tracking your own trend; individual readings are noisy, and no trial shows that lowering a clock score extends life.

## Cognitive Longevity

The brain is not separate from the body. Every physical intervention above also protects the brain. But specific cognitive interventions add additional protection.

**Specific to neurological protection:**
- **Aerobic exercise** → `[E: moderate]` among the best-supported interventions for dementia risk; it increased hippocampal volume in older adults in an RCT (Erickson et al., 2011). Whether adults grow new neurons at all is `[E: contested]`.
- **Sleep** → glymphatic clearance (see above); the non-negotiable
- **Social connection** → `[E: moderate]` weak social ties carry a mortality risk comparable to well-known risk factors (Holt-Lunstad 2010 meta-analysis; the "15 cigarettes" comparison is a popularization, not a finding); social engagement is strongly protective against cognitive decline
- **Cognitive challenge** → `[E: moderate]` lifelong learning, novel skill acquisition, complex problem-solving delays neurodegeneration; "cognitive reserve" — the brain's redundancy built through learning — is protective against clinical manifestation even when pathology is present
- **Omega-3** → `[E: weak]` DHA is a primary structural fat of the brain; fish eaters have larger brain volume (observational), but supplement trials for cognitive decline are mostly null
- **Sauna** → reduces dementia risk independently and synergistically with exercise
- **Hearing protection** → `[E: strong]` hearing loss in midlife is among the largest modifiable risk factors for dementia (Lancet Commission 2020, 2024); protect your ears, and treat hearing loss with hearing aids if it develops

## The Supplement Stack

Evidence-based supplementation — not optimization theater. These have the strongest evidence-to-risk ratios:

**`[E: strong]`:**
- Creatine monohydrate: 3–5g daily. Muscle performance and strength. No need to cycle.

**`[E: moderate]`:**
- Creatine for brain: cognitive benefit under stress/sleep deprivation; growing evidence at standard doses.
- Vitamin D3: 1,000–4,000 IU if blood levels are low. Benefit is in correcting deficiency; replete people saw no cancer or cardiovascular benefit in VITAL. Test; target 30–50 ng/mL serum 25(OH)D.
- Omega-3 (EPA + DHA): 1–2g combined daily if you don't eat fatty fish. Heart-outcome trials are mixed.
- Berberine: 500mg with meals. Lowers glucose and HbA1c in trials of people with diabetes; long-term safety data is thin.

**`[E: weak]`:**
- Magnesium glycinate: 300–400mg before sleep. Worth it if intake is low; sleep evidence is small and mixed.
- Vitamin K2 alongside D3.
- Collagen + Vitamin C: 15–20g collagen with ~50mg Vitamin C before loading exercise; a small trial (Shaw et al., 2017) showed increased collagen synthesis — not yet shown to prevent injury.
- Coenzyme Q10: 100–200mg. Often used for statin muscle pain; trials are mixed.
- NMN/NAD+ precursors: raise NAD+ levels in blood; no human evidence yet of longevity or meaningful metabolic benefit. Sinclair and Patrick supplement; Attia is skeptical. The burden of proof is on the supplement.

## The Centenarian Decathlon

Peter Attia's framework: decide what physical tasks you want to be able to perform at 80, and train backward from that target now.

**Example targets at 80:**
- Carry 30 lbs up a flight of stairs without stopping
- Get off the floor from a seated position without using your hands
- Walk 1 mile in under 20 minutes
- Maintain balance on one leg for 10 seconds with eyes closed
- Have a VO2 max above the 50th percentile for a healthy 50-year-old

Working backward: to perform these at 80, you need to be significantly *above* that level at 50 and 60. Decline is linear if you let it be. It is not if you don't.

**The practical implication:** Your 30s are when you deposit the physical capital that your 60s, 70s, and 80s will draw on. The compounding runs in both directions — building now produces surplus later, and neglecting now creates deficits that cannot be fully recovered.

**Start training for 80 now, regardless of current age.**

## The Annual Longevity Bloodwork Panel

Get this annually. Know your numbers. What gets measured gets managed.

**Minimum panel:**
- Complete metabolic panel (glucose, kidney, liver function)
- Lipid panel with ApoB (request specifically — most standard panels don't include it)
- HbA1c
- hs-CRP
- Vitamin D (25-OH)
- Complete blood count
- Thyroid (TSH, free T3, free T4)
- Iron/ferritin (particularly important for vegetarians and heavy exercisers)

**Add if resources allow:**
- Testosterone (total and free), DHEA, IGF-1
- Homocysteine (cardiovascular risk marker)
- Lp(a) (inherited cardiovascular risk factor — one-time test is sufficient)
- APOE genotype (Alzheimer's risk stratification — one-time test)
- Full hormone panel
- Biological age estimate (PhenoAge or similar)

Review results with a physician who practices longevity medicine or functional medicine, not just a clinician who will tell you your numbers are "normal" when the longevity targets are stricter than standard clinical references.

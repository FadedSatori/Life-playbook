# Longevity Protocols

The goal: not just to live long, but to be physically and cognitively exceptional at 70, 80, 90. Healthspan over lifespan. The interventions with the most evidence, ranked by leverage.

*Built on work from Peter Attia (Outlive), Andrew Huberman, Rhonda Patrick, David Sinclair, and Morgan Levine.*

---

## The Four Horsemen — What Actually Kills You

By Peter Attia's figure, about 80% of deaths among non-smokers over 50 come from four diseases:
1. Cardiovascular disease
2. Cancer
3. Neurodegenerative disease (Alzheimer's, dementia)
4. Metabolic dysfunction (type 2 diabetes and its cascade)

Everything in this file targets the *upstream causes* of these four — years and decades before they manifest. The key insight: these diseases are primarily lifestyle-driven and develop over decades. The interventions that work must be installed decades before the symptoms appear.

**The timing problem:** Most people don't think seriously about longevity until they're sick or watching someone else get sick. By then, 20-30 years of compounding damage has been done. The person who started exercising at 25 will be in a categorically different position at 65 than the person who started at 45, even if they do identical things from 45 onward. Start now, regardless of current age.

## The #1 Longevity Lever: Cardiorespiratory Fitness

**VO2 max** — the maximum rate at which your body can use oxygen during exercise — is the single strongest predictor of longevity across all-cause mortality studies. In Mandsager et al. (2018, about 122,000 patients), the least-fit 25% had about five times the adjusted mortality of the elite (hazard ratio 5.04), and below-average fitness carried about 41% higher risk than above-average. No drug, supplement, or other intervention comes close to this effect size.

**How to build it:**
- **Zone 2 cardio:** 150–180 min/week of low-intensity steady-state (conversational pace, nose breathing possible). Builds mitochondrial density — the engine of every cell. Substrate: fat oxidation. Marker: you can hold a full conversation. Zone 2 is the foundation.
- **VO2 max intervals:** 1–2x/week. 4 min at maximum sustainable effort (approximately 8–9 on perceived exertion), 4 min recovery, 4–6 rounds. This raises the ceiling. Must be uncomfortable.

**Tracking VO2 max:** Wearables (Garmin, Polar, Apple Watch) provide estimates. Lab testing (metabolic cart) is more accurate. For practical purposes, track approximate fitness level against age-adjusted tables. Target: at minimum "above average" for your age. Target: "elite" for your age cohort, which would put you in the top 2.5%.

**The 40-year projection:** Your VO2 max declines approximately 10% per decade after 30, even without intervention. With consistent Zone 2 training, decline can be slowed to 5% per decade. The person who starts with a high VO2 max and maintains the training has cardiovascular capacity at 70 that looks like an untrained 50-year-old.

## Strength Training as Longevity Protocol

Muscle mass is one of the strongest predictors of longevity after 50. Sarcopenia (muscle loss with age) accelerates every disease process. The people who maintain muscle into old age live longer and live better — with dramatically lower rates of metabolic disease, falls, and cognitive decline.

**Targets for longevity (not aesthetics):**
- **Grip strength:** The single best simple proxy for overall muscle health and cardiovascular mortality. A sustained grip below gender/age norms is a strong independent predictor of early death.
- **Hip hinge strength (deadlift pattern):** Protects the posterior chain, prevents falls, maintains power generation from the hips
- **Carry capacity (farmer's carry, loaded walks):** Functional strength that directly translates to real-world capability
- **Overhead pressing:** Shoulder health and functional overhead reach — critical for avoiding the stooped posture of aging
- **Single-leg stability:** Balance, fall prevention, knee and hip health. Falls are one of the primary causes of mortality-accelerating injury in older adults.

**The reverse deceleration:** Muscle is lost at approximately 3–8% per decade after 30 without resistance training. With consistent resistance training, this loss is largely preventable. A 70-year-old who has trained consistently can have the muscle mass of an untrained 40-year-old.

**Minimum effective dose:** 3 sessions/week, compound movements, progressive overload. The key word is progressive — the load must increase over time for adaptation to continue. See [fitness.md](health/fitness.md) for the full periodization protocol.

## Sleep as the Primary Recovery Protocol

Every longevity protocol fails without sleep. See [sleep.md](health/sleep.md) for the full protocol.

**Longevity-specific mechanisms:**
- **Glymphatic system activation:** During deep sleep (N3), the glymphatic system expands and flushes amyloid beta and tau proteins — the precursors of Alzheimer's plaques. Shokri-Kojori et al. (2018, NIH): one night without sleep raised amyloid beta about 5% in the hippocampus and thalamus in 20 people. Chronically insufficient sleep is among the strongest modifiable risk factors for Alzheimer's.
- **Growth hormone release:** The majority of GH secretion occurs during the first slow-wave sleep cycles. Critical for cellular repair, muscle recovery, and metabolic health. Disrupting sleep structure (alcohol is the most common disruptor of slow-wave sleep) suppresses GH.
- **Immune function:** In Irwin's studies, one night cut to about 4 hours reduced NK (natural killer) cell activity by roughly a quarter (to about 72% of baseline). NK cells are the primary defense against cancerous cell proliferation. Chronically short sleep (6 hours or less) is associated with dramatically elevated cancer risk.
- **Telomere maintenance:** Sleep deprivation accelerates telomere shortening — one of the molecular hallmarks of aging.

7–9 hours is not optional. It is medicine.

## Metabolic Health — The Foundation

**Insulin sensitivity** is the metabolic master variable. Poor insulin sensitivity is upstream of cardiovascular disease, cancer, neurodegeneration, and obesity. The alarming statistic: approximately 93% of American adults have at least one marker of metabolic dysfunction.

**What controls it:**
- Exercise (the most powerful lever — muscle is the primary glucose sink; resistance training is particularly powerful)
- Sleep (Spiegel et al., 1999: six nights limited to 4 hours in bed markedly reduced glucose tolerance in 11 young men; "prediabetic" was a later description, not the paper's finding)
- Diet composition (minimize ultra-processed food, refined carbohydrates, vegetable oils; see [nutrition.md](health/nutrition.md))
- Time-restricted eating (8–10 hour eating window — the evidence for longer fasting is thinner)

**Biomarkers to track (annually minimum):**
- Fasting glucose (target: 70–90 mg/dL; risk begins above 100)
- HbA1c (target: below 5.4%; pre-diabetes begins at 5.7%)
- Triglycerides (target: below 100 mg/dL; under 70 is better)
- HDL cholesterol (target: above 60 mg/dL for men, 70 for women)
- Triglycerides/HDL ratio (target: below 1.0 — the best simple insulin resistance proxy)
- Waist circumference (best non-blood metabolic proxy; target: below 37" men, 31.5" women)
- ApoB (the lipoprotein marker that most accurately predicts cardiovascular risk; better than LDL-C; target: below 80 mg/dL for high longevity, below 60 if history of cardiovascular disease)
- hs-CRP (high-sensitivity C-reactive protein; inflammation marker; target: below 1.0 mg/L)

## Hormesis — Stress That Strengthens

Brief, controlled exposure to stressors that activate the body's adaptive response. The biology of antifragility.

**Cold exposure:**
- Cold shower: 2–3 min, 3–4x/week. Activates brown adipose tissue (BAT), improves mood via norepinephrine surge, modest metabolic benefit.
- Cold plunge: the often-cited 11 min/week total (across sessions) comes from what a small group of winter swimmers did (Søberg et al., 2021), not from a tested dose. A reasonable starting point, not a proven target. Šrámek et al. (2000): an hour of head-out immersion in 14°C water raised norepinephrine about 530% and dopamine about 250%. Short plunges haven't been shown to do the same, and the study didn't measure how long the effect lasted.
- Timing: morning cold exposure increases alertness. Avoid immediately post-training — blunts the training adaptation signal.

**Heat exposure (sauna):**
- Laukkanen et al. (2015): 2,315 Finnish men followed a median of about 20 years; 4–7 sessions a week vs. once a week was associated with about 48% lower fatal coronary disease and about 40% lower all-cause mortality. Observational.
- Mechanism: cardiovascular conditioning (heart rate reaches 120–150 bpm), heat shock protein activation (cellular cleanup and quality control), growth hormone release (post-sauna GH is 16x baseline in some studies)
- Post-workout sauna extends the GH spike from training
- Cognitive benefit: sauna use 4–7x/week reduces Alzheimer's and dementia risk by 65% (same Finnish cohort)

**Fasting:**
- Time-restricted eating (16:8) — solid metabolic evidence with minimal downside for most healthy adults
- Extended fasting (24–72 hours, 2–4x/year) — activates autophagy (cellular self-cleaning), mTOR suppression, metabolic reset; requires medical consideration if taking medications or with metabolic disease

## The Hallmarks of Aging

Modern longevity science (Lopez-Otin et al.) identifies 12 hallmarks of aging — the cellular and molecular processes that produce biological deterioration. The lifestyle interventions in this file target multiple hallmarks simultaneously:

- **Genomic instability** — addressed by sleep (DNA repair during sleep), antioxidants, avoiding environmental carcinogens
- **Telomere attrition** — slowed by exercise, sleep, stress management; accelerated by chronic psychological stress and sleep deprivation
- **Epigenetic alterations** — exercise, sleep, and diet quality directly influence epigenetic age (biological age vs. chronological age)
- **Mitochondrial dysfunction** — Zone 2 cardio is the primary intervention; directly builds new mitochondria
- **Cellular senescence** — exercise and fasting both stimulate clearance of senescent cells (zombie cells that drive inflammation)
- **Chronic inflammation** — addressed by diet quality, sleep, exercise, and stress management; the common pathway through which most longevity-shortening behaviors cause damage

The Morgan Levine biological age clock ("Phenotypic Age"): a blood test (phenoAge) estimates biological age from inflammatory and metabolic markers. People who follow longevity protocols consistently run 5–15 years biologically younger than their chronological age.

## Cognitive Longevity

The brain is not separate from the body. Every physical intervention above also protects the brain. But specific cognitive interventions add additional protection.

**Specific to neurological protection:**
- **Aerobic exercise** → the most evidence-backed intervention for Alzheimer's prevention; exercise raises BDNF and supports hippocampal health (whether adult humans grow new neurons is contested)
- **Sleep** → glymphatic clearance (see above); the non-negotiable
- **Social connection** → Holt-Lunstad's 2010 meta-analysis found weak social ties carry a mortality risk comparable to smoking (the "up to 15 cigarettes a day" comparison is an extrapolation popularized by the 2023 US Surgeon General advisory); social engagement is strongly protective against cognitive decline
- **Cognitive challenge** → lifelong learning, novel skill acquisition, complex problem-solving delays neurodegeneration; "cognitive reserve" — the brain's redundancy built through learning — is protective against clinical manifestation even when pathology is present
- **Omega-3 supplementation** → DHA is the primary structural fat of the brain; 2g EPA/DHA/day; brain volume is higher in regular consumers
- **Sauna** → reduces dementia risk independently and synergistically with exercise
- **Hearing protection** → in the 2024 Lancet Commission update, midlife hearing loss ties with high LDL cholesterol as the largest modifiable risk factor for dementia (about 7% each); use ear protection in loud environments

## The Supplement Stack

Evidence-based supplementation — not optimization theater. These have the strongest evidence-to-risk ratios:

**Evidence A (strong):**
- Creatine monohydrate: 3–5g daily. Muscle performance, cognitive benefit under stress/deprivation. No need to cycle.
- Omega-3 (EPA + DHA): 2–3g combined daily. Cardiovascular, neurological, anti-inflammatory.
- Vitamin D3 + K2: 2,000–4,000 IU D3, 100–200mcg K2. 4,000 IU/day is the US tolerable upper intake for adults; don't go higher without testing and a clinician. The 2024 Endocrine Society guideline no longer endorses a target blood level for healthy adults and advises against routine testing (some clinicians still use 40–60 ng/mL).
- Magnesium glycinate: 300–400mg before sleep. Sleep quality, muscle function, 300+ enzymatic processes.

**Evidence B (moderate, growing):**
- Creatine for brain (growing evidence for cognitive benefit at standard doses)
- Berberine: 500mg with meals. Insulin sensitization; mechanism similar to metformin.
- Collagen + Vitamin C: joint health, skin, tendon integrity. 15–20g collagen with 1g Vitamin C prior to loading exercise.
- Coenzyme Q10: 100–200mg. Mitochondrial function; particularly relevant for those on statins.

**NMN/NAD+ precursors:** Growing evidence for metabolic and longevity benefits; cost vs. benefit still being established. Rhonda Patrick and David Sinclair supplement; Peter Attia is more skeptical. Monitor the research.

## The Centenarian Decathlon

Peter Attia's framework: decide what physical tasks you want to be able to perform at 80, and train backward from that target now.

**Example targets at 80:**
- Carry 30 lbs up a flight of stairs without stopping
- Get off the floor from a seated position without using your hands
- Walk 1 mile in under 20 minutes
- Maintain balance on one leg for 10 seconds with eyes closed
- Have a VO2 max above the 50th percentile for a healthy 50-year-old

Working backward: to perform these at 80, you need to be significantly *above* that level at 50 and 60. Decline is linear if you let it be. It is not if you don't.

**The practical implication:** Your 30s are when you deposit the physical capital that your 60s, 70s, and 80s will draw on. The compounding runs in both directions — building now produces surplus later, and neglecting now creates deficits that cannot be fully recovered.

**Start training for 80 now, regardless of current age.**

## The Annual Longevity Bloodwork Panel

Get this annually. Know your numbers. What gets measured gets managed.

**Minimum panel:**
- Complete metabolic panel (glucose, kidney, liver function)
- Lipid panel with ApoB (request specifically — most standard panels don't include it)
- HbA1c
- hs-CRP
- Vitamin D (25-OH)
- Complete blood count
- Thyroid (TSH, free T3, free T4)
- Iron/ferritin (particularly important for vegetarians and heavy exercisers)

**Add if resources allow:**
- Testosterone (total and free), DHEA, IGF-1
- Homocysteine (cardiovascular risk marker)
- Lp(a) (inherited cardiovascular risk factor — one-time test is sufficient)
- APOE genotype (Alzheimer's risk stratification — one-time test)
- Full hormone panel
- Biological age estimate (PhenoAge or similar)

Review results with a physician who practices longevity medicine or functional medicine, not just a clinician who will tell you your numbers are "normal" when the longevity targets are stricter than standard clinical references.

# Hermetic Principles

Seven laws from the ancient Hermetic tradition — attributed to Hermes Trismegistus, preserved in *The Kybalion* (1908), rooted in Egyptian and Greek philosophy. Not mysticism. A set of operating principles that describe how reality is structured. Every serious tradition — Stoic, Buddhist, Sufi, modern systems science — converges on variations of these.

Understand them not as beliefs to adopt but as lenses to see with.

---

## The Seven Principles

### I. Mentalism — "The All is Mind"

*"The Universe is Mental — held in the Mind of THE ALL."*

Everything that exists has a mental component. Consciousness is not a byproduct of matter — it is the medium in which matter appears. The physical world is downstream of thought at every level: personal, cultural, civilizational.

**Modern translation:** Your external reality is a projection of your internal state — not metaphorically, but causally. The patterns in your mind become the patterns in your life. What you believe is possible shapes what you attempt. What you attend to grows. What you ignore atrophies.

**How to use it:**
- Before trying to change any external situation, examine the internal model generating it. Change the model.
- Master the mind first. Physical outcomes are the lagging indicator of mental states.
- The quality of your thinking is the ceiling of your life.

---

### II. Correspondence — "As above, so below; as below, so above"

*"As it is above, so it is below; as it is below, so it is above."*

The same patterns appear at every scale. The structure of the atom mirrors the structure of the solar system. The structure of a cell mirrors the structure of an organism. The structure of an individual mirrors the structure of a civilization. The macro reflects the micro.

**Modern translation:** Cross-domain thinking is not analogy — it is correspondence. The same laws govern all systems at all scales. Learn the pattern at one level, and you've learned it at every level. This is why Munger's latticework works — it is a practical application of the Principle of Correspondence.

**How to use it:**
- When stuck on any problem, ask: what does this look like at a different scale?
- Your relationship with your own body corresponds to your relationship with your environment. Your inner order (or chaos) radiates outward.
- Great leaders are first great self-governors. Build from the inside out.

---

### III. Vibration — "Nothing rests; everything moves"

*"Nothing rests; everything moves; everything vibrates."*

Every particle, every thought, every emotion is in constant motion. What appears solid is matter vibrating at a rate too slow for the eye to track. What appears as emotion is energy moving at a particular frequency. What appears as thought is a pattern in a dynamic field.

**Modern translation:** State is not fixed. Nothing is permanent — not suffering, not comfort, not love, not anger. All states are transitional. The person who understands this does not clutch the good states or resist the bad ones — they ride the wave.

**How to use it:**
- You can change your state deliberately. Breath, movement, cold, music, language — all modulate your vibrational frequency. See [consciousness-technologies.md](consciousness-technologies.md).
- Stagnation is the enemy. Motion — physical, intellectual, creative — is the precondition for aliveness.
- The question is never "how do I make this permanent?" It is: "what is the right state for this moment, and how do I move toward it?"

---

### IV. Polarity — "Everything is dual; opposites are the same"

*"Everything is dual; everything has poles; everything has its pair of opposites; like and unlike are the same."*

Hot and cold are the same thing — temperature — differing by degree. Love and hate are the same emotional energy flowing in different directions. Courage and cowardice are the same impulse, differently expressed. Every quality exists on a spectrum with its apparent opposite.

**Modern translation:** The shadow you run from and the strength you claim are the same energy in different configurations. Anger and passion share a root. Fear and respect share a root. The transmutation of a "negative" quality into a "positive" one is not suppression — it is redirection along the same pole.

**How to use it:**
- Stop treating opposites as separate forces. Find the pole they share.
- Your greatest weakness is likely the shadow side of your greatest strength. Don't eliminate it — transmute it.
- In any conflict, find the polarity. Where two positions seem opposed, the resolution is usually found by moving along the pole, not across it.
- See [shadow-work.md](shadow-work.md) — this principle is the entire basis of shadow integration.

---

### V. Rhythm — "Everything flows; the pendulum swings"

*"Everything flows, out and in; everything has its tides; all things rise and fall."*

Every system oscillates. Day follows night. Expansion follows contraction. Boom follows bust. Enthusiasm follows fatigue. The pendulum swings in both directions with equal force.

**Modern translation:** If you are in a low period, it will pass — not because of hope but because of physics. If you are in a high period, don't mortgage the future against it continuing — it will also pass. The game is to maximize the highs and minimize the impact of the lows, not to eliminate the rhythm.

**How to use it:**
- Design recovery into your training cycles, creative cycles, and life cycles. The rhythm is the mechanism, not the obstacle.
- When you hit a low, don't pathologize it. It's the pendulum completing its swing. Reduce inputs, maintain the foundations, wait.
- When you hit a high, compound it. Use the momentum. The pendulum will swing back; bank the gains while it's in your direction.
- The person who fights the rhythm exhausts themselves. The person who surfs it multiplies their output.

---

### VI. Cause and Effect — "Every cause has its effect; every effect has its cause"

*"Every Cause has its Effect; every Effect has its Cause; everything happens according to Law."*

Nothing happens by chance. What appears as chance is simply cause not yet traced to its source. Every event in your life is the effect of prior causes — some you initiated, some others initiated, some are systemic. The question is never "why did this happen to me?" but "what chain of causes produced this effect?"

**Modern translation:** You are not a victim of circumstance. You are the product of causes — yours and others'. Understanding the causal chain gives you leverage. Change the causes, change the effects. This is the basis of all deliberate development.

**How to use it:**
- Trace every undesirable outcome back to its earliest identifiable cause. Own your contribution. Do not rehearse victimhood.
- Identify the causes that produce the effects you want. Install those causes. Remove the causes that produce effects you don't want.
- Think in systems, not events. An event is the visible surface of a long causal chain.
- The UNIT operates on causes, not events. By the time the event arrives, it's already been shaped.

---

### VII. Gender — "Gender is in everything"

*"Gender is in everything; everything has its Masculine and Feminine Principles."*

Every complete thing contains both generative (masculine) and receptive (feminine) principles. Masculine: initiation, direction, focused projection of force. Feminine: reception, gestation, emergent creation. Neither is superior. Neither alone is complete. All creation requires both.

**Modern translation:** Any act of creation requires both directed will (the masculine principle) and open receptivity (the feminine principle). Too much force without receptivity produces brittle outcomes. Too much receptivity without direction produces drift. The integrated operator knows when to push and when to receive — in relationships, in creative work, in leadership, in life.

**How to use it:**
- In any creative project: alternate between the directed push (masculine) and the open reception (feminine). Ideas need both to form completely.
- In relationships: the capacity to both initiate and receive, to both lead and follow, to both give and accept — this is the mark of the complete human.
- In your own psychology: the integration of both principles is what produces the sovereign individual. The person who is only hard is brittle. The person who is only soft is shapeless.

## Scientific Convergence

The Hermetic tradition is ancient. But it is not obsolete — because every serious modern discipline converges on versions of the same principles.

**Mentalism:** Cognitive science, neuroscience, and psychology all confirm that the brain constructs reality rather than perceiving it directly. Predictive processing (Karl Friston): the brain is primarily a prediction engine, constantly generating and updating models of what it expects to encounter. You don't perceive the world — you perceive your model of the world, updated by sensory input. Change the model; change the perceived reality.

**Correspondence:** Systems science calls this "self-similarity across scales" or fractal structure. The same organizational patterns appear in cells, organisms, ecosystems, and civilizations because the same evolutionary/thermodynamic pressures act at each scale. Munger's "latticework of mental models" is a practical application: learn the pattern at one scale, and you have insight into all scales.

**Vibration:** Quantum field theory describes matter as excitations in underlying quantum fields — everything is, at its foundation, vibrating energy. Neuroscience describes emotional states as physiological patterns (vagal tone, cortisol levels, HRV) that can be deliberately shifted through breath, movement, and environmental design. The Vibration principle is the ancient formulation of what modern psychophysiology confirms.

**Polarity:** Dialectical thinking in philosophy (thesis → antithesis → synthesis), polarity therapy in psychology, the Jungian understanding of the shadow as the opposite of the conscious identity — all describe the same underlying structure. The most stable psychological states are integrated polarities, not suppressed ones.

**Rhythm:** Chronobiology has mapped ultradian rhythms (90-minute cycles of alertness), circadian rhythms (24-hour wake-sleep), and infradian rhythms (seasonal, monthly). Every biological system operates in rhythms. Recovery is not optional — it is the mechanism by which the system consolidates and grows.

**Cause and Effect:** Chaos theory (deterministic systems producing unpredictable outcomes due to sensitivity to initial conditions) and complexity science both confirm: nothing is truly random. Causality runs deeper than visible correlation. The practical implication: trace effects back to causes. The causes can be modified; the effects follow.

**Gender:** Yin/yang in Taoism, Shiva/Shakti in Hinduism, anima/animus in Jungian psychology, the masculine/feminine principles in every wisdom tradition — all describe the same structural polarity. Neither force is superior. Neither alone is creative. All generation requires both directed will and receptive space.

## The Principles in Daily Practice

Abstract principles are useless without application. Here is how to use the Hermetic framework operationally.

**Morning:** Apply Mentalism before engaging with anything external. What is the mental model I'm operating from today? What is the quality of my internal state? You engage the world from wherever you start internally. Set the internal state deliberately before it is set by external input.

**When facing a problem:** Apply Correspondence. What does this look like at a different scale? Where else have I seen this pattern? What domain has solved a structurally similar problem? The answer is rarely in the same domain as the problem.

**When in a low state:** Apply Vibration and Rhythm. This state is not permanent — it is the pendulum completing its swing. The question is not "how do I escape this?" but "what is the rhythm I'm in, and how do I work with it rather than against it?" Apply the state-change tools: breath, cold, movement, sound.

**When in conflict:** Apply Polarity. Find the pole that both positions share. The resolution is almost never on the conflict's surface — it is at the deeper level where both positions are expressions of the same underlying value or fear.

**When making decisions:** Apply Cause and Effect. What causal chain does this decision initiate? What are the second and third-order effects? Who else is in this causal chain? Am I acting on effects I can see, or on causes I can actually influence?

**When facing a significant change:** Apply Gender. What needs to be actively initiated (the masculine principle — directed force, clear intention, decisive action)? What needs to be held open in receptive uncertainty (the feminine principle — waiting, allowing, gestation)? Most people default to one mode. The integrated operator knows which is required.

## The Meta-Pattern

All seven principles are aspects of one reality. They describe the same underlying structure of existence from seven angles.

Read together, they produce a picture: **everything is mind, everything corresponds, everything moves, everything has poles, everything flows rhythmically, everything has causes, and everything contains both generative principles.**

The person who understands this is not surprised by much. They see the deep pattern beneath the surface variation. They know that what appears as chaos is structured. What appears as random is caused. What appears as permanent is transient. What appears as fixed can be changed — at the right level, by the right means.

**The compounding use:** The seven principles are not sequential tools — they are simultaneous lenses. The master practitioner does not apply Vibration or then apply Polarity. They see all seven simultaneously, the way a chess master sees the whole board. That synthesis — moving with the rhythm, working with the polarity, tracing the causality, checking the correspondence — is what distinguishes reactive response from principled navigation of reality.

*"The lips of wisdom are closed, except to the ears of understanding."*

The Hermeticists did not keep these secret out of elitism. They kept them protected because most people, encountering them before they are ready, will misuse or misread them. You encounter them now — which means you are ready to use them.

Use them well.

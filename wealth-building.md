# Wealth Building

[career/finances.md](career/finances.md) covers the defensive and foundational layer: saving, investing, insurance, tax. That layer gets you to security. This file covers the offensive layer: how people build real wealth, not just comfortable savings.

The core claim: **you do not get rich renting out your time.** Salary pays the bills and funds the foundation. Large wealth comes from ownership of assets that grow or produce independent of your hours, and from leverage applied with discipline.

This is general education, not personalized advice. Laws, tax rules, and numbers change. Verify specifics with a qualified professional before acting.

---

## Income vs. Wealth

**Income** is a flow: what you earn per period. **Wealth** is a stock: what you own. Many high earners have little wealth because spending rises with income. Many modest earners build substantial wealth by converting income into assets steadily over decades.

The goal is to move from trading time for money to owning things that earn on your behalf. Salary is the starting capital, not the destination.

**The three stages:**
1. **Earn:** Build skill and income. The fastest early lever. See [career/work-principles.md](career/work-principles.md).
2. **Save and invest:** Convert a large fraction of income into diversified assets. See [career/finances.md](career/finances.md).
3. **Own:** Hold equity in businesses and real assets whose value grows beyond what wages and index returns alone produce.

---

## The Mechanics of Ownership

**Equity** is a share of ownership in something that produces value. It is the common thread of almost every large fortune: founders, early employees with stock, real estate owners, business owners, and long-term holders of productive assets.

Why ownership outperforms wages:
- **Scalability:** A salary is capped by hours. A business or asset can grow without a proportional increase in your effort.
- **Compounding:** Retained and reinvested earnings compound over decades. See the math below.
- **Tax treatment:** In many jurisdictions, long-term capital gains and certain business and real-estate structures are taxed more favorably than wages. Rules vary; get professional guidance.
- **Optionality:** Owned assets can be sold, borrowed against, or passed on.

**Ways to own:**
- **Public equities** via low-cost diversified funds: the accessible baseline that everyone should hold.
- **Private business:** Founding, buying, or taking meaningful equity in a company.
- **Real estate:** Income and appreciation, with leverage and concentration risk.
- **Intellectual property:** Software, books, courses, and media that sell repeatedly.
- **Equity compensation:** Stock or options in a company you work for. Understand vesting, strike price, taxes, and the concentration risk of holding your employer's stock when your paycheck depends on it too.

## Compounding, Honestly

Compounding is the engine, and it is slow at first and then dominant. Illustration with a steady 7% annual return and no withdrawals:

- $10,000 invested grows to roughly $76,000 over 30 years and roughly $150,000 over 40.
- $500 per month contributed for 30 years at 7% grows to roughly $600,000, on only $180,000 of contributions.

Returns are not smooth or guaranteed. These numbers show the shape: **time and consistency do most of the work, and the last decade contributes more than the first three combined.** Starting early matters more than starting big.

**The enemies of compounding:**
- **Interruptions:** Selling in panic, withdrawing early.
- **Fees:** A 1% annual fee difference compounds into a large share of lifetime wealth.
- **Taxes and inflation:** Real return is what remains after both.
- **Large losses:** A 50% loss requires a 100% gain to recover. Avoiding ruin matters more than chasing the maximum. See [antifragility.md](antifragility.md) and the ergodicity discussion in [mental-models/probabilistic-thinking.md](mental-models/probabilistic-thinking.md).

## Leverage: Power and Peril

Leverage multiplies outcomes in both directions. Used well it accelerates ownership. Used carelessly it ends careers and fortunes.

**Good leverage:**
- Fixed-rate, manageable debt against appreciating or income-producing assets, with cash flow that covers payments under stress.
- Operating leverage in a business: high fixed investment, low marginal cost per additional sale.
- Other people's capital and time, used with aligned incentives and clear terms.

**Bad leverage:**
- Debt for consumption or depreciating items.
- Variable-rate or short-term debt against long-term, illiquid assets.
- Margin and derivatives with the potential for losses beyond your capital.
- Debt that requires things to go right to survive.

**Rule of survival:** Never take leverage that a plausible bad scenario can turn into forced selling or bankruptcy. Stress-test: if income fell by a third and the asset fell by a third at the same time, would you still be standing? If not, you are over-leveraged. Survive first; compounding needs you still in the game.

## Building or Buying a Business

Entrepreneurship is the most common path to outsized wealth, and the most dangerous. See [entrepreneurship.md](entrepreneurship.md) for validation, phases, and psychology. Wealth-building specifics:

**Start with a problem people already pay to solve.** Revenue is the proof. Ideas are cheap; paying customers are rare.

**Sales comes first.** A business is a repeatable way of finding customers and delivering value profitably. The founder who cannot sell cannot build. See [sales.md](sales.md).

**Prioritize cash flow and unit economics.** Know the cost to acquire a customer, the margin per sale, and how long cash is tied up. Growth that loses money per customer is a faster way to fail.

**Buying an existing business** is underrated: a profitable small business with a track record removes much of the startup risk. Due diligence on financials, customers, key-person dependence, and the real reason for sale is essential. Use an attorney and an accountant. Avoid overpaying and avoid debt that cannot be serviced in a downturn.

**Build for independence from you.** A business that stops when you stop is a job. Document processes, develop people, and measure the business without you in it. The question from [career/finances.md](career/finances.md): *what must be true for this to run without me?*

**Keep personal risk contained.** Do not put essential savings, your home, or your family's security behind a single venture. The barbell from [antifragility.md](antifragility.md): a secure base, with risk taken in a bounded portion.

## Real Estate

Real estate can build wealth through income, appreciation, debt paydown, and tax treatment. It is also a concentrated, illiquid, management-heavy, leveraged bet. Treat it as a business.

**Before buying an investment property:**
- Compute actual numbers: purchase price, financing cost, taxes, insurance, maintenance (budget a real percentage of rent and value annually), vacancy, and management time or fees.
- Run a stress case: higher vacancy, higher rates, a major repair.
- Know the local market, its job base, and supply dynamics.
- Ensure you can carry it without relying on appreciation.

**Principle:** Appreciation is a bonus, not the plan. If the deal works only if prices rise, it is speculation.

## Investing Principles That Survive

- **Know what you own and why.** Do not buy what you cannot explain.
- **Margin of safety (Graham):** Pay meaningfully less than conservative value, so errors do not ruin you.
- **Circle of competence (Buffett and Munger):** Invest where you have real understanding; be honest about the edge of that circle.
- **Be fearful when others are greedy, greedy when others are fearful**, which is easy to say and hard to do. Pre-commit through rules and automation. See [reality-engineering.md](reality-engineering.md).
- **Diversify what you do not know, concentrate only where you have genuine edge**, and even then size positions so a mistake is survivable.
- **Ignore forecasts and noise.** Nobody reliably predicts markets. Process beats prediction. See [mental-models/probabilistic-thinking.md](mental-models/probabilistic-thinking.md).
- **Avoid get-rich-quick structures.** Guaranteed high returns, urgency, secrecy, and recruitment-driven models are the signature of fraud. Apply [epistemic-self-defense.md](epistemic-self-defense.md) and the scarcity and authority counters in [counter-strategies.md](counter-strategies.md).

## The Wealth Ladder

A rough progression to orient yourself. Move down the list only when the one above is solid.

1. **Stabilize:** Positive cash flow, no toxic debt, emergency fund funded.
2. **Automate:** Savings and investing on autopilot at a high rate.
3. **Diversified core:** Low-cost index funds in tax-advantaged accounts, maxed.
4. **Increase earning power:** Skill, negotiation, and role moves that raise income substantially. See [negotiation.md](negotiation.md).
5. **Add a second engine:** A side business, equity stake, or property that produces independently.
6. **Build or acquire a scalable asset:** A business or portfolio of assets whose value is not tied to your hours.
7. **Preserve and transfer:** Estate planning, protective structures, and a thoughtful plan for giving. See [legacy-at-scale.md](legacy-at-scale.md).

## What Wealth Is For

Wealth is a tool for freedom, protection, and contribution. It is not the mission. The person who accumulates without a purpose becomes a custodian of a pile. The person who knows what the money is *for*, whether it is time, security, a family's future, a body of work, or the capacity to help, uses it with direction. See [purpose.md](purpose.md).

Guard against the traps: status spending, chasing an ever-moving "enough," and letting the pursuit consume the relationships and health the wealth was meant to serve. Bronnie Ware's dying patients did not regret too little net worth. They regretted time. See [time.md](time.md).

# Social Tactics

Specific moves for reading and working with people. High-leverage, low-visibility.

---

## Reading People

**Baseline first.** Before you can read a deviation, you need a baseline. In the first 5 minutes of any interaction, calibrate: What's this person's normal pace, volume, eye contact, and posture? Changes from that baseline are meaningful. The baseline is not.

**Listen for what's not said.** What someone avoids, deflects, or rushes past is often more informative than what they say directly. The omission is the tell.

**Incongruence is the signal.** When the words and the body disagree, trust the body. Microexpressions flash across the face in under 200ms. They're harder to control than words.

**Watch the reaction, not the action.** How someone responds to being contradicted, denied, or surprised reveals their actual character faster than any interview.

---

## Negotiation

**Anchor first, anchor high.** The first number in a negotiation exerts a gravitational pull on everything that follows. Set the anchor before the other side does.

**Silence is power.** After making an offer or ask, stop talking. The first person to fill the silence loses leverage. Discomfort with silence is almost universal — use it.

**Never negotiate against yourself.** If they reject your offer, ask them for their number. Don't pre-emptively move toward them.

**The BATNA principle.** Your Best Alternative To a Negotiated Agreement determines your real leverage. Strengthen your alternatives before the negotiation starts. Walk-away power is the only real power.

**Label, don't argue.** "It sounds like you feel this is unfair" beats "You're wrong." Labeling emotions diffuses them. Arguing amplifies them.

## Persuasion

**People don't change their minds — they find permission to believe what they already want to.** Your job is to give them that permission, not to force a new belief.

**Lead with the outcome they want, not your argument.** Frame every ask in terms of their interest, not yours. "This helps you because..." not "I need this because..."

**Ask questions that lead.** "What would need to be true for this to work?" is more powerful than any argument. Let them construct the case.

**Social proof beats logic.** "Everyone in your position does this" overrides rational analysis in most people. Use it ethically. Resist it consciously.

**The commitment ladder.** Small yeses lead to big yeses. Get someone to agree with something small and true first. The door is open.

---

## Conflict

**The tactical pause.** Before responding to an attack, provocation, or accusation — breathe. The pause breaks the escalation pattern and gives you time to choose your response rather than react. Three seconds feels like forever. It isn't.

**Separate the person from the problem.** You're fighting the issue together, not fighting each other. "How do we solve this?" beats "Here's why you're wrong."

**Reframe to curiosity.** "Help me understand how you got there" disarms defensiveness. Curiosity signals respect. It's almost impossible to stay hostile toward genuine curiosity.

**The broken record.** For unreasonable demands: calmly repeat your position without escalating. Don't defend, justify, or argue. Just restate. Most pressure campaigns collapse when they hit a calm, immovable wall.

---

## Room Presence

- Enter every room like you belong there. Hesitation is visible.
- Take up physical space appropriately — not aggressively, but don't compress yourself.
- Make eye contact when you speak. Break it naturally, not nervously.
- Slow down. Fast speech signals anxiety. Deliberate pacing signals control.
- The person asking the questions is running the conversation. Ask more than you answer.

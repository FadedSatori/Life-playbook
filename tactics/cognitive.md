# Cognitive Tactics

How to think better, focus harder, and perform under pressure. The mind is trainable.

---

## On-Demand Focus

**The entry ritual.** The brain doesn't switch states instantly — it needs a ritual to signal the shift. Build a consistent pre-work sequence (same location, same trigger, same opening action) and use it every time. The ritual becomes the on-ramp. Over time, the ritual alone triggers focus.

**Environment as control panel.** You cannot rely on willpower to maintain focus in a distracting environment. Remove the distraction first, then start. Phone in another room > phone face down. Full-screen app > minimized window. The physical environment is the first variable.

**Time-boxing.** Set a hard end time before you start. Parkinson's Law: work expands to fill available time. A 2-hour open block produces less output than a 90-minute block with a hard stop. The constraint is the accelerant.

**The 5-minute rule.** If you can't start, commit to just 5 minutes. Most resistance is start resistance, not sustain resistance. Once you're in, inertia works for you.

---

## Stress Inoculation

**Voluntary discomfort.** Cold showers, hard workouts, fasting, difficult conversations — deliberate, controlled exposure to discomfort raises your ceiling. You cannot perform calmly under pressure you've never encountered. Each exposure trains the nervous system.

**The challenge-threat distinction.** Physiologically, challenge (I can handle this) and threat (this is going to hurt me) produce different responses. Challenge state improves performance; threat state degrades it. The reframe is: *"I am prepared for this. My body is ready."* Say it before high-stakes events. It changes the biochemistry.

**Manufactured urgency.** Simulate pressure in training. Set shorter deadlines than needed. Add observers. Raise the stakes artificially. When the real pressure arrives, it's familiar.

## The Inner Monologue

Elite performers have disciplined self-talk. Most people don't control it at all.

**Replace "I have to" with "I get to."** Language shapes state. Obligation produces resistance; privilege produces energy.

**Process cues over outcome cues.** Under pressure, think about what you're doing, not how you're doing. "Stay low, drive forward" beats "Don't mess this up." The brain executes process instructions; it chokes on outcome anxiety.

**The name-it-to-tame-it technique.** Label negative emotions precisely as they arise. Not "I'm stressed" but "I'm feeling anxious about the outcome." Precise labeling engages the prefrontal cortex and reduces limbic reactivity. You get distance from the emotion without suppressing it.

**Third-person self-talk.** When facing self-doubt, speak to yourself by name: "What does [Name] do here?" Psychological distance from the threat improves clarity and performance. Used by athletes, military operators, surgeons.

## Pattern Recognition

Intuition is pattern recognition at speed. It's built through deliberate exposure, not passive experience.

**Debrief every significant event.** Ask: what patterns led here? What did I see or miss? What would I watch for earlier next time? The debrief is how raw experience becomes encoded knowledge.

**Cross-domain study.** Patterns that appear in one field (military strategy, chess, biology) often appear in others. Study history, read outside your domain, collect case studies. The broader your reference library, the faster you pattern-match.

**The pre-mortem as pattern training.** Before a project: imagine it has failed spectacularly. Work backward. What went wrong? You're not predicting — you're rehearsing the pattern of failure so you can recognize it earlier when it starts to emerge.

**Calibrate your confidence.** Track your predictions. Score your decisions. The gap between how certain you feel and how often you're right is your calibration error. Narrow that gap over time.

---

## Cognitive Resource Management

The brain depletes. Treat it like a battery.

- **Decision fatigue is real.** The quality of decisions late in the day is lower than early. Schedule high-stakes choices for your peak window.
- **Monotask.** Switching between tasks has a cost. Batch similar work. Single-task on anything that requires depth.
- **Input quality controls output quality.** Garbage in, garbage out applies to the mind. What you consume (news, social media, conversations) shapes your baseline cognitive state. Curate aggressively.
- **Recovery is not optional.** Mental performance requires recovery the same as physical performance. Sleep, walks, downtime, boredom — these are not wastes of time. They're the recharge that makes the next deep session possible.

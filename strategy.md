# Strategy

Long-game thinking. Not what to do next — where to be positioned so the right moves become obvious.

---

## Positioning

The best move is often made weeks or years before it's needed.

- **Be in the room before the opportunity.** Build relationships, skills, and credibility before you need them. Reactive positioning is always weaker.
- **Own the high ground.** In any competitive situation, ask: which position gives me the most options? Occupy that. Options are the currency of strategy.
- **Make yourself hard to replace.** Rare + valuable beats common + competent. Find the overlap of what you're good at, what the world needs, and what almost no one else can do.
- **Avoid single points of failure.** In income, relationships, health, reputation — concentration creates fragility. Diversify the critical systems.

---

## Asymmetric Leverage

Look for moves where effort is small and the effect is large. These are always available — most people aren't looking.

- **The 80/20 rule applied ruthlessly.** 20% of inputs produce 80% of outputs. Identify the 20% and over-invest there. Cut the rest.
- **Compounding is the most powerful force in any domain.** Skills, reputation, relationships, wealth, knowledge — all compound. Start early. Interrupt the compounding as rarely as possible.
- **Trust is asymmetric leverage.** Earned slowly, destroyed instantly. A high-trust reputation opens doors that talent alone cannot. Invest in it.
- **Ask better questions than everyone else.** The person who frames the question often controls the answer. Question quality is a strategic advantage.

---

## Patience as a Weapon

Most people are playing a short game. The long game has less competition.

- **Time arbitrage:** Be willing to wait when others can't. The impatient always pay a premium — to the patient.
- **Let problems solve themselves.** A large percentage of urgent problems are not problems in a week. Don't spend capital on what time will resolve.
- **Compound your patience.** The longer you can hold a correct position, the larger the eventual payoff. This applies to investments, relationships, and ideas.
- **Recognize the late-mover disadvantage.** Others will start more things, generate more noise, burn more resources. Stay focused. Let them tire.

---

## Narrative Control

The story being told about you — by you and others — shapes what's possible.

- **Frame first.** The person who defines the terms of the discussion usually wins it. When entering any negotiation or conflict, name the frame before the other side does.
- **Reputation precedes you.** Invest in what people say about you when you're not there. That version of you opens doors you'll never know about.
- **The story you tell yourself matters most.** Identity narratives (I am someone who...) are the most powerful behavioral governors. Choose them deliberately.
- **Silence is strategic.** You don't have to fill every gap. Comfortable silence is a power move. Over-explaining is a tell.

---

## The Endgame Principle

Work backward from where you want to end up.

- **Define "enough."** For money, status, achievement. Without a finish line, the race never ends and you never win.
- **Don't optimize for the metric that replaced the goal.** The map is not the territory. Revenue is not the business. Likes are not influence. Keep the actual goal in view.
- **Legacy question:** At the end, what do you want to have built, done, and been? Let that pull present-day decisions toward it.
